# Figure legends

**Figure 1 | An expanding community contributing to PRIDE.** **a–b,** Diversity increased among contributor groups (×5.0), journals (×5.4) and instrument models (×1.8), and decreased among species (×0.67). Effective number (Hill number) combines the number of categories with their relative shares among PRIDE datasets. a, order 2 (inverse Simpson) by release year relative to 2016. b, 2023–25 vs 2013–15 at orders 2, 1 and 0 (category counts adjusted to equal dataset counts); 95% intervals from resampling contributor groups, except for contributor-group diversity, which is descriptive. A dataset naming several categories contributes equally to each. **c–d,** Four in ten public datasets come from groups depositing for the first time. PRIDE datasets per release year, grouped by the year the contributor group first deposited; groups are defined from hashed principal-investigator contacts. **e–g,** Europe supplied half of PRIDE’s 3.3-fold growth; dataset counts grew 5.2× for China and 2.8× for Germany. Circle area: datasets released 2023–2025, by the contributing group’s country; colour: multiple of the 2016–2018 count (archive-wide 3.3×). 3,632 and 12,085 datasets with an assigned country (78% of each period; the rest have no country assignment). Datasets added: Europe 4,218 (50%), North America 1,852 (22%), Asia 1,848 (22%; China 1,283). Counts describe deposits, not instrument ownership or national research output.

**Figure 2 | A growing archive spanning different experimental scales.** **a–c,** Typical datasets hold half as many raw runs as in 2013–16, yet the number with ≥100 runs rose from 58 in 2013 to 531 in 2025. PRIDE datasets released 2013–2025 with ≥1 identified raw run or raw bundle (n = 35,141 of 36,249). Runs are instrument acquisitions stored in vendor raw formats; bundled files make these counts lower bounds. Runs are not samples: TMTpro-18plex tandem mass tags can combine 18 samples in one run. Ridges: distribution per release year (equal area, log axis); white tick = median. Pooled medians 30 (2013–16, n = 2,498) and 16 (2023–25, n = 15,248); 95% intervals from resampling groups defined by hashed principal-investigator contacts. ¹ Thermo Fisher, no detected data-independent acquisition (DIA) or TMT, human samples, linked publication. ² Runs with fraction numbers combined into one count as an approximation to sample count. ³ Bruker 2013–16 n = 56. Grey: the confidence interval for the ratio includes 1. **d,** Median raw-run size is 9× larger for the Orbitrap Astral than for the LTQ Orbitrap. Median size of one raw run by instrument model (bars: interquartile range), placed at the model’s first year in PRIDE. **e–f,** 1% of datasets hold 24% of the archive’s 2.71 petabytes. Cumulative share of PRIDE bytes held by the largest datasets (all deposited files, 2013–2025).

**Figure 3 | A public record of instruments and analysis workflows.** **a–b,** Deposits named new instrument models alongside older ones. a, Share assigned to each selected model among datasets with an instrument-model label; a dataset naming several models contributes equally to each. Every model named in ≥50 datasets still appeared in 2025 releases; deposits include delayed releases and reanalyses. b, Effective number of models (inverse Simpson index of the weighted model shares); shading: 95% intervals from 300 bootstrap resamples of contributor groups. **c,** Within five years, DIA-NN became the third most frequently named analysis program in public datasets. Share of PRIDE datasets naming each analysis tool (protocol text or software field), by release year. **d,** Software is named more often in deposits from its developers’ country: 18 of 20 tool–country pairs. Frequency of each program among groups in its developer’s country relative to all countries combined (1 = equal frequency); 95% intervals from resampling contributor groups. Median 1.8×; permutation p = 0.0002.

**Figure 4 | Results shared in standard and software-specific formats.** **a–b,** The share of datasets with standard result files ranges from 0.2% to 76% across analysis programs. PRIDE datasets naming a single analysis program and containing submitter-deposited mzIdentML or mzTab files; programs with ≥60 datasets. Repository-generated files are excluded. **c–d,** Reading deposited outputs reveals identifiable results in 80% of 2025 datasets; 16% contain standard result files. PRIDE datasets released in 2025 (n = 6,231) with identification or quantification results recognised from file names, archive contents and table headers. **e,** README documentation and sample-to-file mappings in SDRF files recur within contributor groups more often than expected from their overall frequencies. Share of a group’s next PRIDE dataset carrying each file when the previous one did, vs the rate expected for datasets of the same years.

**Figure 5 | Connecting public deposits to the research literature.** **a,** At public release, the median time since acquisition was 2.2 years and the software release was at least 3.4 years old. Age at public release of the latest raw-file acquisition date (datasets with dated raw-file names, 34% of those with raw files), the sequence database date and the software release (first seen in PRIDE; lower bound). The plot excludes one negative database interval and two negative software intervals; ages above 10 years are pooled in the final bin. **b–c,** Proteomics journals carried 51% of dataset-linked papers in 2013 and 10% in 2025. Journals of linked papers, obtained from Europe PMC using PubMed identifiers: 22,475 papers published 2013–2025 (525 without a journal, mostly preprints, excluded). Journals ranked by linked datasets; small line plots show each journal’s share of papers per year, counting each paper once. Proteomics journals = title contains “proteom” (18 titles). Bands: 95% bootstrap over contributor groups. **d–e,** Qualifying papers more often name human datasets and datasets with data-independent acquisition (DIA); standard result files show no detectable association. PRIDE datasets released 2013–2022 (n = 20,684) mentioned by identifier in a qualifying paper other than their primary reference, during the release calendar year or the next three calendar years (2.3% overall). Qualifying papers name datasets from ≥2 contributor groups and no more than 100 datasets; publication and release order within a year is unresolved. Thermo Fisher only means a single-vendor assignment from instrument labels. Rates are standardised to release year and human status; odds ratios use the adjusted model in Methods. Europe PMC text searches miss some dataset mentions, and a mention does not establish that the data were analysed again. Open circle: SDRF files were often added after release by reuse projects (91 of 205 datasets predate the format), so the association’s direction cannot be determined.


# Supplementary figure legends

**Supplementary Fig. 1 | Datasets and storage concentrated among contributor groups.** **a–c,** The top 1% of contributor groups released 18% of PRIDE datasets and 38% of its bytes. 36,244 datasets released 2013–2025 from 13,892 contributor groups (hashed principal-investigator e-mail/ORCID; top 1% = 139 groups; 5 datasets without one principal investigator). Bytes = all files listed per dataset. Gini index of inequality in dataset counts: 0.42 in 2013–17 and 0.49 in 2021–25. Lines in c: 95% bootstrap over groups.

**Supplementary Fig. 2 | Deposit history of common instrument models.** **a–d,** All 37 common models still appear in 2025 deposits. Rows: models named in ≥50 public PRIDE datasets released 2013–2025 (35,770 datasets with model labels; each dataset contributes equally to its named models), ordered by first year with ≥3 datasets. Ridge height = √(share of that year’s model-labelled datasets), common scale. Thin line = years with ≥1 dataset. For rows starting in 2013, earlier appearances fall outside the study period. An instrument label does not establish ownership; its first recorded year does not establish a purchase date.

**Supplementary Fig. 3 | First deposits naming new instrument models, by continent.** **a,** Africa’s 6-year delay is close to that expected from its 69 datasets. First release year of 15 models (≥100 datasets; first ≥5 in 2014 or later) in each continent, based on principal-investigator country (28,225 of 36,249 datasets). Delay is measured from each model’s first deposit anywhere and summarised as the median across models observed. Ring: expected median first year if instrument labels were independent of continent within each year, given each continent’s dataset count. A deposit is not a purchase record. † Hypergeometric comparison assumes independent datasets. It ignores clustering within contributor groups, making p-values too small (≈4.5 of 90 pairs expected below .05 by chance).

**Supplementary Fig. 4 | Software version series in deposited files.** **a–e,** Among 2025 MaxQuant deposits with a recoverable version, 40% used the 1.6 series, first seen eight years earlier. Black triangles: the first record of a version series by a second contributor group. Grey numbers: datasets with a version. 2025 coverage: MaxQuant 361 of 576 datasets naming the tool, Proteome Discoverer 112 of 486, DIA-NN 110 of 331, FragPipe 45 of 142. a–d: submission (deposit) year on one time scale; 2025 = submissions public by 31 Dec 2025. e: 95% group-bootstrap intervals over hashed contributor groups.

**Supplementary Fig. 5 | Ages of software releases and sequence databases.** **a,** At public release, median software-release age is ≥5.2 years for Mascot and ≥1.4 for Skyline. PRIDE datasets released 2013–2025 whose deposited files record an exact software release (one value per dataset and tool). Age = public release date minus the first PRIDE submission recording the same release. Software exists on or before that date, so every age is a lower bound. Using the first record by a second contributor group instead gives median ages of Skyline 1.2, FragPipe 1.5, DIA-NN 2.1, MaxQuant 3.3 and Mascot 4.9 years. Excluded: 2 datasets whose PRIDE release date precedes the first-seen submission; 4 Skyline files whose "version" was "64-bit". **b–e,** Deposited sequence databases are typically 1.7 years old, and 42% are older than two years. PRIDE datasets released 2013–2025. b, c: age of the main sequence database (FASTA) at submission, from a high-confidence date in its file name (2,558 datasets, 1,305 groups; median 1.67 years, 95% confidence interval 1.58–1.80; >1 year 69%, >5 years 8%). A name date may be a download, release or tool build date, so ages are approximate.
